Stri Purush Tulana (A Comparison between Women and Men), published in 1882, was written by Tarabai Shinde, a Maharashtrian woman associated with the Satyashodhak circle.

It was written in response to the Vijayalakshmi case, in which a Brahmin widow was convicted for infanticide, and it turned the public condemnation of the woman back on the men and the social order that produced her situation.
The tract argues that the vices attributed to women are found equally or more in men, and that the difference lies not in nature but in the licence society grants to one sex and denies the other.
It is regarded as the first modern feminist text in Marathi, and among the earliest such arguments in any Indian language, written by a woman with no formal education beyond home instruction.
Its circulation was small and it was largely forgotten until rediscovered in the twentieth century, with Jyotiba Phule among the few contemporaries who defended it publicly in Satsar.
